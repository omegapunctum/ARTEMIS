## Bounded question and subject

Test whether ARTEMIS can communicate one artifact, two attributed and unresolved scholarly dating assertions, separate provenance and the consequences of disagreement without a winner or false precision. Subject: exactly one artifact, Codex Atlanticus `ATL.1466.1 / fol. 733 verso`; the exact **Claim subject** is now bounded in the [owner-accepted research package](2026-09-24_ATL_1466_1_CONFLICT_EVIDENCE_PACKAGE_v1.md). Two principal positions only: Heydenreich `c.1490` and Pedretti `c.1514–1515`. These are source-native scholarly expressions, not established production dates. Each position needs its own reviewed Claim, EvidenceLink and reproducible locator. Direct scholarly provenance must be distinguished from attribution mediated by the Museo Galileo catalogue. The possible two-period use of the sheet stays conditional, attributed and secondary; it is not a third dating Claim or proof that both dates are true.

## Semantic and presentation requirements

- Preserve one canonical artifact identity and two separately attributed alternative temporal Claims. Existing Claim/Evidence/Uncertainty and renderer-neutral state/projection contracts own meaning. No new fundamental `Conflict` object, Relation, duplicate object, UI-owned truth or generalized conflict engine.
- Expose to a novice before technical provenance disclosure: the artifact identity, **Dating disputed**, both named positions and their differing chronological implications. Progressive disclosure provides each Claim's locator, attribution, reasoning where supported, uncertainty and the conditional two-period qualifier. Do not require internal vocabulary to understand the disagreement.
- Neither position may become canonical through order, color, size, chronology position, publication recency or interpretation focus. Focus on either interpretation is reversible presentation/query state, not mutation; the other position remains discoverable.
- Keep source-native `circa` wording; distinguish approximate uncertainty *within* each Claim from unresolved disagreement *between* Claims. No exact-year or closed numerical bounds, `±N`, midpoint, synthesized `1490–1515` interval, continuous uncertainty band, probability, source score or automatically selected winner.
- Show two conceptual source-native alternatives for the **same artifact** without promising deterministic membership in calendar Range, Scrub or normalized timeline filtering. Existing Leonardo Range/Scrub behavior remains unchanged. Case-specific Range membership, Scrub entry/accumulation and normalized query coordinates are outside v1; they would need a separate accepted decision plus evidence.
- Retain a fair same-content conventional scholarly prose comparator for later human evaluation: same identity, positions, provenance access, qualifiers and limitations. Do not weaken the comparator or claim user value without actual comparative novice evidence.

## Research gate and execution boundary

Before historical-data or runtime promotion, Research/curation had to close only the following (accepted in [package v1](2026-09-24_ATL_1466_1_CONFLICT_EVIDENCE_PACKAGE_v1.md)): (1) one exact common bounded Claim subject; (2) reproducible Museo Galileo locator(s); (3) honest direct-vs-mediated provenance; (4) two separately reviewed dating-attribution Claims; (5) reviewed EvidenceLinks/locators for each; (6) correct existing epistemic classification and source-conflict uncertainty; (7) source-native `circa` wording; (8) reviewed provenance for the conditional two-period-use qualifier. No numerical query envelope is required for v1. Research's earlier `STOP_CONFLICT_PROOF_V1` under the *previous* envelope prerequisite did not test common-subject or evidence suitability and is not a historical/evidence failure of this case. The [accepted package](2026-09-24_ATL_1466_1_CONFLICT_EVIDENCE_PACKAGE_v1.md) closed that evidence gate before implementation; later implementation is recorded in the [technical closeout](2026-09-24_EPISTEMIC_CONFLICT_IMPLEMENTATION_CLOSEOUT_v1.md).

After evidence closure, any bounded implementation must preserve Claim identity/attribution/provenance across canonical state and projection, maintain non-canonical reversible interpretation focus, and retain existing Leonardo, Region, route-null, localization and accessibility behavior. Human presentation/interaction review is required before an implementation `REVIEW` PR merges. Technical/browser checks and publication do not validate comparative or formal user value.

Verification for a later implementation must establish one artifact identity throughout canonical state and projection, two independently attributed Claims and evidence paths, unresolved source conflict without a synthesized date, reversible non-canonical focus, and reproducible locators that do not turn mediated provenance into direct attribution. Browser and human review must inspect default dispute presentation, both interpretation focuses, provenance disclosure and narrow layout without implying either position wins. Those were the implementation acceptance checks; [PR #453](https://github.com/omegapunctum/ARTEMIS/pull/453) later merged after owner presentation acceptance. This specification does not claim browser transcript evidence or user-value validation. Range-only and Scrub-only case scenarios from the pre-amendment handoff have been superseded for v1.

Later comparative novice work must use fresh participants and the same questions against ARTEMIS and a competent same-content prose baseline: artifact identity, both attributions, what is disputed, chronological alternatives, what remains unestablished, whether a combined date is justified and where each evidence locator can be inspected. Record unaided correctness, assistance, critical epistemic errors, provenance attribution, use of temporal consequences, cognitive load and learning/order effects. No simulated participant result or automatic VALUE_SIGNAL.
