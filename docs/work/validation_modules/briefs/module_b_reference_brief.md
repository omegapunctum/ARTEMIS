## Question

How did function, material and institutional program reshape architectural form within post-war modernism, and which links between housing, transport and cultural buildings are actually documented?

## Compared Features

- **Unité d’Habitation, Marseille** — Provides the named housing precedent for Park Hill and an official account of the vertical-city program.
- **Park Hill, Sheffield** — Tests how a documented continental precedent was adapted to a municipal slum-clearance program, steep site and community-preservation objective.
- **Preston Central Bus Station and Car Park** — Adds integrated transport infrastructure whose official record names broad Le Corbusier influence but not a specific source building.
- **Royal National Theatre** — Provides the institutional and multi-auditorium parent complex without treating every part of it as derived from Epidaurus.
- **Olivier Theatre** — Provides the exact auditorium endpoint for the National Theatre archive's explicit Epidaurus influence statement.
- **Theatre of Epidaurus** — Supplies the explicit auditorium precedent named by the National Theatre archive and prevents the comparison from reducing program to material style.

## Conclusion

Status: `provisional`

Program is not a secondary label in this set: interior streets, integrated transport and auditorium organization shape the selected modern works. Two transmissions are documentable with exact endpoints—Park Hill from Unité and the Olivier Theatre from Epidaurus—while Preston's broader Le Corbusier attribution must remain agent-level and unresolved at the building level.

## Uncertainty

- Park Hill's official record documents intent more strongly than resident outcomes.
- The Park Hill–Unité relation needs a reviewer to test whether “based on” is best normalized as modelled_on or adapted_from.
- Preston must not be linked to Unité solely because both evidence sets mention Le Corbusier.
- The Epidaurus relation uses the Olivier Theatre as its exact endpoint rather than the whole National Theatre.
