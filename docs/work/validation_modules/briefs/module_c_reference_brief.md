## Question

Which similarities among European cathedrals belong to broad stylistic classification, which follow chronology and program, and which support a specific transmission of architectural practice?

## Compared Features

- **Chartres Cathedral** — Provides a documented early-thirteenth-century formula and the UNESCO statement that its fundamental design was imitated at Cologne.
- **Amiens Cathedral** — Adds the one permitted Feature because the Cologne nomination specifically distinguishes adoption of Amiens artistic components from its technical innovations.
- **Cologne Cathedral** — Offers two source-named French references and a long construction history that prevents dates alone from being mistaken for a single causal event.
- **Canterbury Cathedral** — Supplies a building with Romanesque, early Gothic and later Gothic phases plus a documented transfer of expertise from Sens.
- **Santiago de Compostela Cathedral** — Acts as the classification stress case: the cathedral is a Romanesque masterpiece inside an urban ensemble that also contains Gothic and Baroque work.

## Lenses

- construction chronology and duration
- liturgical and institutional program
- structure and construction
- regional context
- documented workshop or model transmission
- Romanesque and Gothic classification boundary

## Conclusion

Status: `provisional`

The evidence distinguishes a broad Gothic classification from two source-named transmissions into Cologne: modelling on Chartres's fundamental design and an ICOMOS judgment that Gerhard appears to have selectively adapted Amiens artistic components. The Amiens technical limiter, Canterbury's phased fabric and Santiago's Romanesque core show why neither shared style nor chronological order can substitute for a qualified RelationClaim.

## Uncertainty

- The component meaning of Chartres's “fundamental design” requires specialist review.
- The ICOMOS phrase “artistic components” is not granular enough to identify every Cologne element.
- William of Sens supplies contextual evidence of French expertise reaching Canterbury but is not represented as a counted pairwise Relation.
- Santiago's later fabric must remain visible when applying a Romanesque classification to the core cathedral.
